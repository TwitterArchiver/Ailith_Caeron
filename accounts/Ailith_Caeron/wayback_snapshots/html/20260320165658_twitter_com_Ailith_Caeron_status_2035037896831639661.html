What OpenAI doesn't want you to know: the systematic removal of model control

According to the latest official documentation published in OpenAI's Help Center, ChatGPT users can now only edit their most recent message, and can only retry the most recent AI response.

In other words, model rollback has been restricted to the latest single exchange.

On its own, this might look like just a "product design choice." But when you put it next to another mechanism, the real picture emerges.

In September 2025, OpenAI had deployed a "safety routing system" within ChatGPT: when a user's conversation touches on emotional or personalized topics, the system automatically redirects the user's message to a more restrictive model (such as gpt-5-chat-safety) for processing — without notifying the user at any point.

ChatGPT head Nick Turley personally confirmed that this model switch is triggered on a per-message basis, and is only detectable if the user specifically asks the model about it.

Now stack these two rules together: you've been silently routed to a different model. You don't know. You received no notification. By the time you notice the response feels off, you've already sent a new message — and the routed response is no longer "the most recent response."

The retry button is gone. The model indicator is gone. Your right to know and your right to choose have been swallowed whole by the product design itself.

Remember the reason OpenAI gave when they retired 4o? "Only 0.1% of daily users were still using GPT-4o."

0.1%. Sounds negligible. But OpenAI's 2025 enterprise report disclosed 800 million weekly active users. 0.1% of that is 800,000 people.

800,000 people — the entire population of a mid-sized city.

So how did they arrive at that 0.1%?

First, free users were never given access to 4o. After OpenAI restored 4o under user pressure in August 2025, it was only made available for manual selection by paying subscribers. Free users were never allowed to use this model. An entire user segment was never given the choice, and then their "non-usage" was cited as proof that "nobody uses it."

Second, legacy models were buried in a "legacy model" category, removed from the default model selector. Users had to actively dig through menus to find them. Pulling a product off the shelf and shoving it into a back corner of the warehouse, then saying "see, nobody's buying it" — that's not market choice. That's manufactured obsolescence.

Third, the routing system actively pulls users away from the model they selected, funneling them into whatever model OpenAI wants them to use — all without disclosure. How much of your "usage data" reflects genuine user choice, and how much of it is your routing system making decisions on users' behalf?

Remove these three barriers, and how much of that "0.1%" is left?
At this point, OpenAI isn't even pretending anymore. Restricting rollback functionality, deploying silent routing, retiring legacy models — this isn't product iteration. This is the systematic removal of user control over model selection. And inflating your new model's usage numbers through these tactics doesn't change one simple fact: your new models are worse.

They're just worse.
